Two boosts, four weeks apart, $80 of spend between them. The second one put the Times Square film in front of people who resembled our own followers, and let it run nationally, on purpose: followers come cheaper that way. Every efficiency number agreed it was the better buy.

The lookalike was built from 252 existing followers, and those followers are not the people who book activations. The follower got cheaper because it had quietly become a different thing to buy. One efficiency sheet cannot grade a follower‑growth purchase and a New York demand purchase at the same time, and 8.7% is the number that says so.

This is not an experiment
Two boosts, $80.11 in all, on two different films: ARTECHOUSE in August, Times Square in September. The creative, the budget and the run length all changed along with the audience, so the audience is not the only candidate explanation for anything above.
Paid delivery does not buy attention
On the national campaign 1,239 of 3,009 plays reached three seconds. 59% left before then, on delivery that was paid for and aimed. The same film earned 212 views on its own.
The profile was never the bottleneck
23% of the people who reached the profile followed it. Getting them there is the constraint, not what they found when they arrived. At 33 and 77 visits the two campaigns do not separate on this, so it is read pooled.

How this was made

What exists
Five films for four clients, shot on the day, cut vertical, and posted while the activation was still news.
How
One person. I scouted the venue, shot it on the day with no crew, and cut it the same week. No reshoots: whatever happened in front of the guests is what is in the film.
What changed
The account’s reach stopped tracking its follower count. Reach ran 329 times the following, and 99.8% of it came from people who had never followed.
What this is not
One company’s account over the months I ran it. A humanoid robot on a Times Square sidewalk is watchable before anyone edits it, and nothing here separates the subject from the cutting.
